# Broker / depot price-list changes since a date

Use when the user asks something like "How did the price list for X change since Y?", where X is a broker, depot or bank offering (e.g. a neobroker, a direct bank depot, a tariff such as FREE or PRIME+) and Y is a date. The user is a long-term investor in Germany: mostly ETF savings plans, plus occasional one-off ETF purchases.

## Step 1 - Pin down X and Y
- Confirm the exact provider **and tariff or price model**. Providers often have several tariffs, and a change may affect only one. If unclear, ask, or cover all tariffs and label them.
- If Y is vague ("last year", "since I opened my account"), turn it into a concrete date and say which date you used. If it's missing, ask.
- Note today's date. You are comparing the state on date Y with the state today.

## Step 2 - Get the CURRENT price list
- Find the provider's current official **Preis- und Leistungsverzeichnis** (price and service list), Konditionen or Preise page, or PDF. Prefer the provider's own documents to third-party summaries.
- Record its version or "Stand" date and URL.
- Also check the provider's FAQ or help centre for fee details that aren't in the main list (venue surcharges, savings plan rules, interest).

## Step 3 - Reconstruct the price list AS OF DATE Y
Try these sources in order, and record which one gave each figure:
1. **Archived copies of the provider's own pages or PDFs** in the Wayback Machine (web.archive.org/web/<YYYYMMDD>*/<url>). Pick the snapshot closest to, but not after, date Y. Old PDF price lists often stay online under versioned file names, so search for them too.
2. **The provider's own announcements**: press releases, blog or product-news posts, "Änderung der Bedingungen/Preise" notices, app release notes.
3. **Independent tests with a fixed cut-off date**: Stiftung Warentest/Finanztest depot cost tables (they give a "Stand" date) and Verbraucherzentrale market checks.
4. **Reputable comparison sites and news** that date their figures (finanztip.de, justetf.com broker pages, handelsblatt.com, wiwo.de, finanzfluss.de, broker-test.at and similar). Treat undated pages with caution.
5. **The user's own documents**: old price-list PDFs, change-notification emails, confirmation of terms. If the evidence is thin, ask the user whether they have any. These are often the most reliable source.

If you can't establish a fee's value at date Y, say "unknown at Y". Never guess.

## Step 4 - Build a timeline of changes between Y and today
List each change with its **effective date** and **announcement date**, if known. There may be several changes in between (e.g. a fee introduced, removed, then reintroduced). Note the reason given, e.g. the EU payment-for-order-flow ban effective mid-2026, new venues, or tariff restructuring.

## Step 6 - Impact on the user
- Recalculate the user's **annual cost at Y vs now** for their typical usage. Ask, or reuse from the conversation: monthly savings plan amount, number and size of one-off orders per year, venue used, cash held. Show the difference in EUR.
- Call out explicitly:
  - any fee that switched from flat to **percentage-based**, or any new percentage fee. These scale with the money and compound, so they matter most for long-term investors;
  - savings plans that stopped being free;
  - a venue becoming more expensive (suggest the cheaper venue if one exists);
  - lower interest or reduced deposit protection;
  - restrictions on outgoing transfers.
- Note the user's rights:
  - For changes to existing contracts, providers must announce them in advance, typically at least two months ahead for contract changes.
  - Since the 2021 BGH ruling (XI ZR 26/20), banks generally can't rely on silence as consent for significant changes.
  - A depot transfer to another provider is free of charge from the sending bank.
  - Keep this short, and tell the user to check the provider's notice for exact deadlines.

## Step 7 - Report
1. One-line summary: overall cheaper, the same, or more expensive for your usage, by EUR X per year.
2. The before/after table.
3. A timeline of changes with dates.
4. Impact and recommendations (e.g. switch venue or tariff, or consider moving).
5. Gaps: fees you couldn't verify for date Y.
6. Sources, each with its date or snapshot date.

If the user asks about several providers or dates, give one table per provider and a final comparison line.
